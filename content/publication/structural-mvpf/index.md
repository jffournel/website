abstract: >-
  <div style="text-align: justify">

  Which climate investments should policymakers prioritize, and how much should they spend? We develop a framework combining the marginal value of public funds (MVPF) with structural models of demand for green technologies to evaluate the welfare effects of environmental reforms. Our approach traces the MVPF across the entire policy spectrum using counterfactual simulations, thereby relaxing the constant elasticity and ``small change'' assumptions of sufficient-statistics methods. We apply our method to electric vehicles and public charging in Quebec, residential solar in Connecticut, and a fuel-economy label experiment in the United States. At the margin, public charging and the fuel-economy label deliver higher returns than purchase subsidies or taxes. The returns to subsidies fall with spending, whereas the returns to public charging and the fuel-economy label do not. For programs with increasing returns, the optimal reform is all-or-nothing, and the implementation depends on the return of the whole program rather than on the return of the marginal dollar spent.
Keywords: climate policy, sufficient statistics, demand models, non-marginal reforms, environmental externalities.
